# Post 1: Framework (Tuesday)

Your CEO doesn't need more updates. They need the right updates.

Most technical leaders either over-communicate (drowning executives in sprint details) or under-communicate (radio silence until crisis mode).

Here's the 5-bullet template I send every Friday:

1. **Wins** - 1-2 things that shipped or went well
2. **Progress** - Key metrics against our agreed milestones
3. **Blockers** - What's slowing us down (if anything)
4. **Decisions needed** - What I need from you this week
5. **Looking ahead** - What's coming next week

Takes 5 minutes to write. 2 minutes to read.

The magic is in "decisions needed" - it shifts the dynamic from "reporting to boss" to "partnering on priorities."

# Post 2: Industry Take (Wednesday)

The problem isn't your delivery. It's your silence.

I've seen CTOs get fired despite solid technical execution. I've seen others survive major project failures.

The difference? Communication.

Here's what most technical leaders miss:

You know exactly what's happening on your team. Your stakeholders know nothing. This asymmetry means your comfortable silence feels ominous to them.

A week without updates becomes "I wonder if there's a problem."

Two weeks becomes "There's definitely a problem."

A month of silence means "We need to bring in someone to figure out what's going on."

The engineer's default - "I'll surface issues when they arise" - reads to executives as "I'll tell you when it's too late."

Proactive communication isn't about managing perception. It's the price of autonomy.

Would you rather hear "You're updating me too much - just flag the big stuff" or "Can you send me a status update?"

The first earns trust. The second invites scrutiny.

# Post 3: Personal Lesson (Thursday)

The best career advice I ever got: "It's better to be told to stop communicating than to be chased for updates."

Early in my leadership career, I kept my head down. Shipped reliably. Thought results would speak for themselves.

They didn't.

I still got chased for status updates. Still got pulled into "quick check-ins" that consumed hours. Still felt micromanaged despite delivering consistently.

What changed everything:

I started sending a one-paragraph update every Friday. Here's what happened, here's what's coming, here's what I need.

For the first two months, my CEO read every word. Asked follow-up questions. Requested more detail.

By month three, shorter replies.

By month six: "You don't need to copy me anymore. Just flag anything I should know about."

That sentence is worth more than any promotion. It means you've earned autonomy.

Managing up isn't politics. It's building the trust that lets you do your best work without constant oversight.

What communication habit has earned you the most trust?
